Once there was a small library at the edge of a busy town. Every morning, Mira
opened the windows, dusted the shelves, and wrote down which books people had
borrowed. She noticed patterns. Children returned adventure stories quickly but
kept books about stars for many days. Gardeners asked for weather books after
long dry weeks. Travelers always wanted maps.

One rainy afternoon, a boy came in and asked for the book with the blue cover
that explained why the moon changed shape. Mira remembered seeing it near the
window, between a book about tides and a book about calendars. She found it at
once. The boy smiled because the answer had been waiting in the right place.

By winter, Mira had learned that a library was more than a room full of paper.
It was a memory for the whole town. Some memories were exact, like a title or a
name. Other memories were softer, like knowing what kind of story someone might
need before they knew how to ask for it.
